Prologue

Eight years ago…

The rainy half-dark beyond the panoramic windows was giving way, grudgingly, to the heavy golden light of a setting sun. At this hour the Vesper casino felt like a sleeping temple of the shadow world — vast, magnificent, suspended in the brittle, almost reverent silence that comes only before a storm.

Fifteen-year-old Isabella moved slowly between the empty gaming tables. Her footsteps sank into the deep pile of the burgundy carpet. She trailed her fingertips along the polished wood rim of the roulette wheel, studying the perfect sectors of the disc with quiet fascination — the alternating black and red, the endless circular chase.

The air was thick with an expensive perfume: wax on antique furniture, the finest leather, the faint notes of sweet Cuban tobacco, and the lingering trail of her grandfather's cologne.

That smell had always meant absolute safety to her.

"Do you like it here, Isabelle?" The low, endlessly warm voice of her grandfather made her turn.

Vittorio Romano stood at the tall window, hands clasped behind his back. In his immaculate grey three-piece suit, a silver watch on a chain, he looked like the patriarch of a vanished era — a man whose name made judges and politicians go pale on both sides of the ocean, yet who now watched his granddaughter with a soft, almost sad smile.

"It's very beautiful, Grandfather," Isabella answered quietly, drawing closer. "But somehow... frightening. At night, when the chandeliers blaze, it's loud and lively. Right now it feels as though the walls are watching us."

Vittorio laughed softly, the sound echoing under the high vaulted ceilings. He came to her and touched her cheek with his dry, warm palm in a fatherly gesture, tucking a strand of dark hair behind her ear.

"Your intuition doesn't deceive you, my girl. The walls of Vesper truly see everything and forget nothing. Remember: this is not merely a place where people lose fortunes. It is a theatre. A stage on which the powerful of this world shed their masks and reveal their true, ugly faces. And whoever owns this house must be stronger than all their sins combined."

Isabella frowned, searching his face. For a moment she thought she glimpsed a shadow of deep, long-worn exhaustion behind his eyes.

"But you own it," she said stubbornly. "So you're the strongest of all?"

"I simply keep the rules of this game under control," Vittorio replied with an enigmatic smile, shifting his gaze to something over her shoulder. "But rules change. And young predators always come to replace old wolves."

"Vittorio, the documents from the Swiss bank have arrived. They're waiting for your signature."

The voice that came from the shadows of the second-floor gallery made Isabella's heart skip a beat. It was remarkably even, deep and velvety, yet threaded through with a cold, metallic hardness.

Isabella turned and held her breath.

A young man was descending the wide curved staircase — slowly, without a trace of youth's restlessness in his movements. He was tall and lean, in a black silk shirt with the top buttons carelessly undone and sleeves rolled to the elbows. A thin, neat scar whitened on his left cheekbone, a mark of some recent wound that only sharpened his predatory, dangerous beauty.

His eyes — cold as grey glass that had set hard — swept the room and settled on Isabella. There was no warmth or courtesy in that gaze. He looked at her the way one assesses a potential threat. Or a potential value.

"Adrian," Vittorio nodded in greeting, and Isabella noticed with surprise that her grandfather spoke to this young man as an equal. "Come closer. I want you to finally meet my granddaughter. Isabelle, this is Adrian. The man who understands this business better than anyone else. My finest pupil and my right hand."

Adrian closed the distance between them without a sound. When he stopped a couple of paces away, Isabella felt a cold, dangerous energy radiating from him. He smelled of rain, bitter coffee, and something elusively metallic.

"A pleasure, Isabella," he said, dipping his head slightly. His voice sent a strange, unsettling tremor through somewhere deep in her chest.

"Likewise," she answered, lifting her chin stubbornly and forcing herself not to look away from his steel eyes. She was only fifteen, but she had already learned to hide her fear behind a mask of pride. "Grandfather says you control the rules."

A barely perceptible, almost ghostly smile touched Adrian's lips. He dropped his gaze to her slender fingers still resting on the roulette cloth, then looked directly into her face again.

"Rules exist to make guests feel safe, Princess," he said quietly, taking an almost imperceptible step forward. "But real power begins where rules end."

"Adrian is right," Vittorio interjected gently, watching them both with barely concealed interest. "But Isabelle finds it too quiet here during the day. She thinks Vesper is asleep."

Adrian shook his head slowly, never taking his piercing gaze from Isabella — a gaze that stopped her breath.

"Silence is deceptive, Isabella," his voice dropped to a half-whisper, vibrating in the empty hall. "This place never sleeps. It simply waits for its hour. And… some places choose people long before people choose them."

He turned and moved towards Vittorio's office with a light, predatory stride. Isabella watched him go, feeling a strange, frightening warmth spread through her. That warm evening at sunset, she did not yet know those words would become a prophecy. Or that eight years later this cold man would be her only chance to survive the hell that was about to open beneath her feet.

Chapter 1

Theatre of the Dead

POV: Isabella Romano

Rain in this city did not wash away the filth — it only made it glitter.

Isabella pulled her black wool coat tighter, feeling the damp, biting cold work its way under her thin silk blouse.

She was twenty-three, and until today her world had been simple, legible, and predictable. It consisted of dusty university lecture halls, endless stacks of books on comparative linguistics, seminars on semiotics, and a rented flat with a permanently dripping tap.

She had just finished her degree in linguistics, fluent in five languages. Isabella was accustomed to dissecting words, hunting hidden meanings in ancient texts, decoding dialects and catching the finest shades of intonation. She had thought her future lay in translation, academic papers, and a calm, measured life.

All these years she had been convinced that her grandfather, Vittorio Romano, had sent her abroad to study after the car accident that killed her parents purely for her own safety.

He paid her bills, sent elegant cards at the holidays that smelled of expensive tobacco, and never failed to repeat: "Live your life, Isabelle. Don't look back." She hadn't looked back. She had believed he ran some modest, legitimate establishment on the city's outskirts and was quietly living out his days in peace.

Until three days ago, when she was informed that Vittorio Romano was dead. Heart failure.

And now she stood in an old private cemetery wedged between the grey concrete walls of a megalopolis, watching a lacquered coffin descend slowly into the wet earth.

But the coffin wasn't the worst of it.

The worst was the people who had come to see Vittorio off.

There were about thirty of them. All men. Not a single woman but Isabella herself. Every one dressed in a perfectly cut black suit from the finest Italian tailors. Yet there was no grief in their postures. Nobody wiped away a tear. They stood in a dense, silent wall, chins held high, hands deliberately at their sides or hidden in overcoat pockets.

Isabella stood out against this grim backdrop. At 1.73 metres she was not a fragile slip of a girl, but among these broad-shouldered men she seemed like a delicate flower. Bright copper curls spilled out from beneath her deep black hood. On her pale, cold-flushed face a scatter of fine golden freckles showed clearly, and her enormous, unnaturally blue eyes glittered with unshed tears. She looked like a living flame dropped into a crypt by mistake.

She noticed how heavily their jacket fronts hung. Weapons — concealed under expensive fabric. Isabella, trained to read non-verbal signals, understood: these people had not come to say goodbye to a friend. They had come like vultures circling a cooling carcass, ready to tear into one another at any moment over the inheritance.

"You don't belong here, Isabella."

The low, velvet voice came from behind her so suddenly she flinched. It instantly sent a flash of memory through her — the golden afternoon light in an empty casino hall, the click of a roulette wheel, cold grey eyes in a young man's face.

She turned sharply.

The man who stood before her under a large black umbrella seemed like a living embodiment of this merciless day. Pure Italian, extraordinarily tall — no less than 1.88 metres — broad-shouldered, in a flawlessly fitted black overcoat that emphasised his athletic frame.

His face might have belonged to a fallen angel — features too perfect, too sharp, too predatory, a strong jaw, and icy grey eyes in which not a single living emotion stirred. A thin, pale scar crossed his left cheekbone. He was thirty-two. The age at which a man already possesses absolute power and undisputed authority, while remaining unmarried and free of all attachments.

Adrian Vincenzo.

Her grandfather's right hand. The man the late Vittorio had spoken of with reverence.

His grey eyes moved across her copper curls, paused on her bright blue eyes and her freckles, and narrowed. His gaze held no sympathy. Only a hollow, appraising void.

"I came to bury my grandfather," Isabella answered quietly but firmly, forced to tilt her head well back to meet his eyes. She lifted her chin stubbornly, promising herself she would show no weakness to this man. "I have every right to be here."

"You came to a slaughter, Princess," Adrian stepped closer, making her feel the full difference in their height and power. He smelled of wet asphalt, bitter coffee, expensive wood and gunpowder. "Look at them. These are not grieving relatives. These are the heads of syndicates who obeyed your grandfather only because they feared him more than the devil himself. But Vittorio is dead. The moment the solicitor opens the will, the redistribution of power begins. And if you get in their way, they'll crush you and not even notice. Run. Go back to your quiet Switzerland before this soil soaks through with fresh blood. That is my advice to you. The only one I'll give for free."

"I'm not leaving until I've heard the will," she said flatly.

Adrian studied her face in silence for a moment. A flicker of irritation mixed with a strange, dangerous interest crossed his features before he snapped his expressionless mask back in place.

The reading of the will was set for Vittorio Romano's old mansion outside the city — a vast, forbidding house of dark stone ringed by ancient oaks whose bare branches were clawing at the grey sky.

Inside, it was half-dark. Heavy velvet drapes had been drawn tight. Isabella was led into an enormous library with a high fireplace hall at its centre. A massive oak table stood in the middle, behind which sat the elderly solicitor, Mr Bianchi, whose hands trembled visibly as he arranged his official papers.

Around the table, on leather sofas and in armchairs, sat the same men from the cemetery.

Three of them stood out.

First: Marco Vargas, a heavyset man of about fifty with a crushing stare. Second: Leo Kowalski, a trim financial magnate with a banker's cold eyes. Third: Adrian Vincenzo himself, standing slightly apart, shoulder leaning against a bookcase, hands casually pocketed — yet his gaze controlled every centimetre of the room.

When Isabella entered, conversation stopped at once. Dozens of heavy stares converged on her.

Marco Vargas smirked, running a slow, measuring look over her figure. He turned to his aide and, making no effort to conceal his contempt, spoke in a rough Sicilian dialect:

"Questa ragazzina dai capelli rossi sarà facile da piegare. Le prenderemo tutto prima del tramonto."

(This red-haired girl will be easy to break. We'll take everything from her before sunset.)

Vargas was certain the girl raised in a Swiss boarding school would not understand a word.

Isabella did not betray herself with so much as a flicker of muscle. The linguist within her registered the Sicilian dialect, translated the phrase, and filed it away in the deepest drawer of her memory.

She simply turned her head slowly to Vargas. Her bright blue eyes narrowed to two chips of pure ice, and her gaze became so direct, so heavy and penetrating that Vargas stumbled mid-word.

She said nothing. But that look from beneath her copper fringe made the man dimly sense: this was no lamb.

Adrian Vincenzo, watching her carefully from his corner, caught that split-second of micro-expression. His grey eyes darkened with woken curiosity.

Mr Bianchi swallowed convulsively, adjusted his glasses, and tore open the stiff envelope sealed with the Romano family wax seal.

"I, Vittorio Romano, being of sound mind and firm memory…" Bianchi began reading. His voice wavered slightly.

Everyone in the room leaned forward. The atmosphere became incandescent. The men were certain that Vittorio's empire — the legendary Vesper casino — would be divided among them. Vargas was already smiling in anticipation of his share.

"…bequeath all my personal savings, all real property, and one hundred per cent of the shares of the Vesper gaming house together with all associated management rights within the Syndicate…" Bianchi paused, his face going pale. He raised a frightened look to the assembled company.

"Get on with it, old man!" Vargas barked.

The solicitor swallowed and read the rest of the sentence:

"…to my sole direct heir and granddaughter. Isabella Romano. Full and indivisible right of ownership passes to her from the moment of my death."

A dead, deafening silence fell over the library once more.

Isabella felt the floor shift beneath her feet. He had left her absolutely everything? It was madness. It was suicide.

The next second the silence exploded.

"What is this rubbish?!" Vargas lurched to his feet, his heavy chair screeching back. "Has the old fool completely lost his mind?! Hand Vesper to a girl who doesn't even know the rules of blackjack?! We will not let some copper-headed doll destroy what we have been building for years!"

"It's a mistake," Kowalski said quietly but with menace. "The girl won't last a week. The competition will simply remove her, and we lose the territory. This is unacceptable."

The men in the room erupted, voices merging into a single roar. Several figures moved towards Isabella. They were looking at her the way people look at an inconvenient obstacle that needs to be removed by the most radical means available.

Isabella stepped back half a pace, her heart hammering wildly. The door was blocked by Vargas's men. She was trapped.

"Enough."

A single word, spoken in a low but astonishingly deep and icy voice, cut all noise dead.

Adrian pushed himself off the bookcase and took several steps forward, silencing everyone. His movements were fluid, almost languid — but that ease was threaded through with the lethal power of a predator about to spring.

He stopped between Isabella and the other men, shielding her with the width of his shoulder. He was a full head taller than her, and his massive frame radiated absolute control.

"The will is entirely lawful," Adrian said calmly, holding Vargas with his steel gaze. "Vittorio Romano made this decision in full possession of his faculties. And anyone who attempts to contest his wishes… will answer to me."

"You're protecting her, Adrian?" Vargas squinted. "She's taking what belongs to you by rights. Or have you decided to get cosy with the girl for a warm berth?"

In the same instant Adrian was beside Vargas. The movement was so fast Isabella couldn't follow it. Adrian's fingers locked around the large man's throat, pressing him effortlessly into the heavy oak wall of the library.

A dull thud. Vargas choked out a cough. His bodyguards reached simultaneously for the weapons under their jackets — but at that same moment three of Adrian's men rose silently at the library doors with submachine guns levelled at the opposition's chests.

"Watch your tongue, Marco," Adrian said quietly, almost gently, leaning close to Vargas's face, his grey eyes narrowing. "I am the manager here. I answered to Vittorio, and I will answer to his lawful heir. If you have a problem with that — say so now, to my face. But remember: those will be your last words in this house."

Vargas swallowed convulsively, his eyes widening with animal fear. He raised his hands slowly.

Adrian released him with a look of contempt. Vargas slid down the wall with a groan, gulping air.

Adrian turned slowly to face the rest of the room. Under his heavy, glacial gaze, not one person dared to make a sound. Even Kowalski looked away.

"That will be all for today," Adrian announced coldly. "Mr Bianchi will handle the paperwork. You may all go."

The men began leaving the library in silence, casting heavy, promise-of-retribution looks at Isabella as they went.

When the great library door finally clicked shut, leaving them alone, Isabella felt her knees trembling. She walked slowly to the table and leaned on it with both hands, trying to steady herself.

"Why did he do it?" she whispered, staring at the documents spread before her. "Why did Grandfather leave me all this? He knew… they would want to kill me."

Adrian moved towards her slowly. Now, with no outsiders in the room, his cold mask had grown harder still. He stopped two paces away, looking down at her with a mixture of contempt and a grim, resigned weight.

"He left it to you because you are a Romano," he replied coldly. "His blood. He hoped his strength would awaken in you. But what I see before me is a frightened girl who trembles at the sight of a firearm."

Isabella straightened sharply. Adrian's words cut deeper than all of Vargas's fury combined. Her bright blue eyes blazed out from beneath her copper curls, and an angry flush rose on her cheeks, right over her golden freckles.

"I didn't ask for this inheritance!" she exclaimed, staring up at his ice-cold face. "And I didn't ask you to protect me! If you think me so weak — why didn't you simply let them kill me?"

Adrian took another step, closing the distance between them to almost nothing. He loomed over her with his full height and mass until she could feel his burning breath.

"Because twenty years ago your grandfather saved my life," he said quietly, almost ominously, holding her eyes. "And I am repaying that debt. The debt only, Isabella. Don't expect compassion, understanding or warmth from me. I will protect your life because I promised Vittorio I would. But if you are foolish enough to put yourself in the path of a bullet — I will not weep over your body."

He reached past her and took one of the documents from the table, holding it out to her.

"What is this?" she asked.

"Your disclaimer," Adrian answered levelly. "Sign it. All rights pass to me as chief manager. You receive your millions in your Swiss accounts and leave on the first flight out. You will be safe, and you will never hear the word Syndicate again. Sign it, Isabella. If you want to live — this is your only chance."

Isabella looked at the document. Neat lines of text that could return her to her former, peaceful life. A life where she need not fear every shadow or meet the cold eyes of a man who despised her fragility.

She reached out. Her fingers touched the stiff paper. Adrian watched her movements carefully, waiting for her to relent — waiting for her to confirm his opinion of her as a soft, sheltered girl.

That thought struck somewhere in Isabella's chest with a sharp, stubborn ache. Romano blood ran in her veins. She would not let Adrian Vincenzo and the rest of the vultures trample her grandfather's last wish.

Isabella raised her eyes to Adrian slowly. In her bright blue gaze lit that same defiant, unbending flame he had noticed eight years before.

She tore the document in half. Then in half again — into small pieces. The fragments drifted down like white petals onto the dark wooden floor at her feet.

"I won't sign any disclaimer, Adrian," she said firmly, holding his gaze. "My grandfather trusted this place to me. And I am staying."

Adrian was silent for several seconds, staring at the scraps of paper at his feet, then raised his eyes to her. His grey eyes darkened to the colour of a storm sky, and the muscles of his jaw worked beneath the skin. He exhaled slowly, as if holding back a rising tide of something, and took one last, decisive step towards her.

"Stubborn, foolish girl," he breathed, almost too quietly — and his voice dropped into low, vibrating Italian that stopped her breath and sent a shiver down her spine: "Allora da oggi appartieni a questa casa. E che Dio ti aiuti, principessa, perché io non lo farò."

(Then from today you belong to this house. And God help you, Princess — because I won't.)

He turned and strode out of the library, leaving her alone among the ruins of her former life. Outside the mansion windows the wind was howling, hurling heavy drops of rain against the glass, and Isabella knew: there was no road back. The game had begun. And the first stake had already been placed.
